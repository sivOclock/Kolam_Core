Lesson 0 · The transformer, box by box

The picture on the left is the famous figure from the 2017 paper Attention Is All You Need. GPT, BERT, chat models and our kolam model are all built from these same boxes. We climb it from the bottom, one box at a time, each with a small example. The picture stays beside you and lights up the box we're on. Click any box to jump to it.

the transformer, 2017

Two towers, a bridge, and a picker

The transformer was invented to translate. So our example, all the way up, is a tiny translation:

theblackcat → lechatnoir

French: le = the, chat = cat, noir = black. French puts the colour after the animal. Keep that in mind for box 11.

Left tower · the reader
the encoder. Reads “the black cat”, all words at once.
Right tower · the writer
the decoder. Writes “le chat noir”, one word at a time.
Arrows between · the bridge
the writer looks at what the reader understood.
The top · the picker
turns the writer's result into “next word: noir”.

The colours tell you each box's job:

pink · turn tokens into numbers orange · look at other tokens yellow · safety rails blue · think alone purple, green · pick the next word

Order: the left tower first (boxes 1–7), then the right tower (8–12), then the top (13–15). Every box ends with in our kolam model: where that box lives in kolam_feed/model.py.

1Inputsbottom of the left tower

Chop the input into tokens

A computer can't read words. So the sentence is cut into pieces called tokens, and each token gets an ID number from a fixed list, the vocabulary.

simple example
the#5 black#1043 cat#2368

(ID numbers made up.) The paper's vocabulary had about 37,000 tokens. Rare words get cut into smaller pieces: playing → play + ing.

in our kolam model Our input is a picture, not words. The model cuts the kolam image into a 24 × 24 grid of patches: 576 “tokens”, one per 16 × 16-pixel square of the 384 × 384 image.
The amber square is one patch = one token. SeqModel.memory(), model.py:111.
2Input Embeddingleft tower · pink

Turn each token into a list of numbers

Each ID is looked up in a big table. Out comes a vector: a list of numbers that describes the token. The numbers in the table are learned in training.

simple example

Pretend each vector had only 3 numbers, and we could name them: animal? colour? small word?

Similar meaning → similar numbers → nearby points: cat lands next to dog, black next to white. Real vectors have 512 numbers (in the paper), and nobody names them. Training decides what each number means.

in our kolam model The “table” for the image is the CNN eye (ResNet-18, pretrained on photos). It looks at each patch and writes 256 numbers about what's drawn there: a curve, a crossing, a dot, bare floor. Encoder, model.py:43.
3⊕ Positional Encodingthe ⊕ circles

Add a “where am I” stamp

The next box, attention, compares tokens but has no sense of order. So every vector gets a position code added to it. That's the ⊕.

simple example · the problem

simple example · the trick: clock hands

The paper's code works like a clock with several hands: a fast one, a slower one, a very slow one. Every position gets its own set of hand angles:

Look at pos 0 and pos 6: the fast hand points the same way, but the middle hand doesn't. Together, no two positions look the same, and neighbours look alike. Each angle becomes two numbers (its sin and cos), and those are simply added to the token's vector. The paper uses 256 hands, from fast to very, very slow.

in our kolam model Image patches get the same clock trick twice: once for the patch's row, once for its column (_pos2d, model.py:75). Written tokens get a different stamp: “I'm writing encoding row 3, column 5”, so the writer knows exactly which gap it's on (box 9).
4Multi-Head Attentionleft tower · orange

Every word looks at every other word

The heart of the transformer. Each word asks a question, every word offers an answer, and each word mixes in the answers that fit.

simple example

cat asks: “is any word describing me?” Every word says how well it matches:

the
10%
black
60%
cat
30%

So cat takes 10% of the's numbers, 60% of black's and 30% of its own (vectors from box 2):

That mix is mostly colour: “black” has flowed into the cat's slot. Box 5 adds it onto the cat, and the cat's slot now means black cat.

the three names: query, key, value
Query · Qwhat I'm looking forlike what you type in a search barcat: “a word describing me”
Key · Kwhat each word says it islike a video's titleblack: “I describe things”
Value · Vwhat each word hands overlike the video itselfblack: “the colour black”

Match the query against every key → scores → softmax makes them percentages (box 14) → mix the values by those percentages. Unlike a search engine you don't get one result: you get a blend.

attention = softmax( Q · Kᵀ / √d ) · V

Q · K is a “dot product”: multiply pairwise and add. It's big when two vectors point the same way. Lesson 2 does it by hand.

why “multi-head”

One look isn't enough. The box runs 8 lookups side by side (“heads”), each with its own Q, K and V, each hunting for something different. Like 8 friends reading the same sentence, each asked a different question. Made-up but typical heads for it:

The 8 results are glued back together into one vector.

in our kolam model Model A has no left tower: the CNN eye does the reading. Model B (the reader) uses 2 layers of exactly this box over the 576 patches, so every patch knows about the whole kolam (model.py:194). Our heads: 8, each 32 numbers wide (256 ÷ 8).
5Add & Normevery yellow box

Two safety rails after every box

After each orange or blue box comes a yellow one. Add: keep the original and add the change. Norm: bring the numbers back to a steady size.

simple example · add (the shortcut)

Still mostly “animal”, now with a strong “colour”. It's track-changes, not a rewrite. Without the shortcut the cat's slot would be just [.27, .57, .15], more colour than animal: the cat starts to vanish.

In the picture it's the line that goes around each box into the yellow one (lit green right now). It's called a residual connection. It also lets the learning signal flow back through deep stacks.

simple example · norm (auto-volume)

Numbers can drift very loud or very quiet as they pass through many boxes. LayerNorm rescales each vector to average 0 and spread 1: same shape, steady size. Move the sliders:

going in
after LayerNorm

The input jumps around; the output doesn't move. (LayerNorm also has two learned knobs, a scale and a shift, so the model can choose a different steady size.)

in our kolam model Same two parts, slightly different order: we Norm before each box and Add after it (norm_first=True, model.py:106), which trains more steadily, plus one last Norm at the very top (model.py:108).
6Feed Forwardblue boxes, both towers

Each word thinks on its own

Attention is a meeting: words share information. Feed Forward is the desk work after it: every word goes through the same small network, alone.

simple example

Think of the middle layer as a row of yes/no detectors (names made up; real ones aren't labelled). The cat's vector goes in, each detector gives a number, and ReLU keeps the positives and turns negatives into 0:

Only the detectors that fire pass a message on; the result is added back onto the word (Add & Norm). Size in the paper: 512 → 2048 → 512, spread out 4× wider, then squeezed back. It's the biggest single part of each layer, and much of what a model “knows” is stored here.

in our kolam model 256 → 1024 → 256 (the 4 * d in model.py:106). The same desk routine for every token.
7N×the grey frames

Stack the same layer N times

Everything inside a grey frame is one layer. The model stacks N copies, each with its own numbers. The output of one is the input of the next.

simple example

Like reading a tricky sentence several times. What the vector in the slot of it could mean after each layer of the black cat drank because it was thirsty:

after layer 3“it” = the thirsty black cat
↑
after layer 2“it” = the cat
↑
after layer 1“it” is in the “because …” part
↑
start“it” = some pronoun

The paper stacks 6. GPT-3 stacks 96. Same layer, different height. At the top of the left tower you get one rich vector per input word, and those cross the bridge to the writer (box 11).

in our kolam model The writer has 4 layers (layers=4, model.py:96). Model B's reader has 2.
8Outputs (shifted right)bottom of the right tower

The writer reads its own answer so far

The right tower's input is the answer written so far, pushed one place to the right, with a <start> token in front.

simple example

Each slot sees the words before its target and must guess the target. In training we already know the whole answer, so all four slots run at once (box 10 keeps that fair). When translating something new, we don't know the answer: write one word, feed it back in, repeat (box 15).

in our kolam model Target 0 0 | 6 1 0 | …, input <bos> 0 0 | 6 1 …. Literally the targets shifted right: torch.cat([BOS, tgt[:, :-1]]), model.py:126. (The code calls the start token <bos>, “beginning of sequence”.)
9Output Embeddingright tower · pink, and its ⊕

Same two steps on the writing side

Each written token → its vector from a second table (for the output language) → plus its position stamp. Boxes 2 and 3 again.

simple example

The French table has a row for le, chat, noir, <start>, <end> and every other French token. Slot 3 gets the vector of “chat” plus the stamp for position 3.

in our kolam model Our table has just 13 rows: 0–8, |, <end>, <bos>, <pad>, each 256 numbers (self.tok, model.py:103). And the stamp isn't “slot #7” but which gap of the encoding the slot writes: its row and column, counted from the | tokens so far (token_slots, model.py:85). The first slots of the Lesson 1 kolam:

row = how many | so far; column = tokens since the last |. Counting from 0, like the code.

10Masked Multi-Head Attentionright tower · first orange box

Same attention, but no peeking ahead

The writer's first box is box 4 again, with one rule: a word may look only at itself and the words before it.

simple example
rows = the slot asking · ✓ = may look at

Why: in training the whole answer sits in the input (box 8). Without the mask, the slot that must write noir could look one step ahead, see “noir” and copy it, learning nothing. With the mask it only sees <start> le chat: a fair test. Every slot is a fair test, so one pass trains all of them at once.

How it blocks: forbidden scores are set to −∞ before softmax, and softmax turns −∞ into exactly 0% (try it in box 14).

try it · the same sentence as box 4, now with the mask
in our kolam model One line: torch.triu(torch.full((L, L), float("-inf")), 1), −∞ above the diagonal (model.py:121). GPT uses exactly this mask.
11Multi-Head Attentionright tower · the bridge

The writer looks across at the reader

The writer's second orange box. Same maths again, but the question (Q) comes from the writer, and the keys and values (K, V) come from the top of the left tower: the two arrows crossing over.

simple example · click a French word

in our kolam model Q comes from the token being written (“I'm row 1, column 0”), K and V from the 576 image patches (memory() goes in as mem: self.dec(x, mem, …), model.py:122). So while writing a gap's digit, it should learn to look where that gap is drawn. Pick a token:

12Add & NormFeed ForwardN×the rest of the right tower

The rest of the writer: you know it already

Add & Norm after every box (box 5), one Feed Forward (box 6), and the whole layer stacked N times (box 7).

one writer layer, as a recipe
  1. look back at the words written so far (masked attention) → Add & Norm
  2. look across at the reader (cross-attention) → Add & Norm
  3. think alone (feed forward) → Add & Norm

Repeat N = 6 times in the paper.

in our kolam model nn.TransformerDecoderLayer(d, heads, 4 * d, 0.1, …, norm_first=True) with d = 256 and 8 heads, stacked 4 times (model.py:106–107). The 0.1 is dropout: while training, randomly zero 10% of the numbers so the model can't lean on any single one.
13Lineartop · purple

One score for every word in the vocabulary

The writer's final vector is compared with a pattern for every word in the vocabulary. Each comparison gives a score, called a logit. High score = likely next word.

simple example · slot 3, which has seen “<start> le chat”
final vector → compare with each word's pattern →

Scores can be any number, even negative. They're not percentages yet. It's the same “how well do we match” idea as attention's Q · K, but against every word at once. The paper: 512 numbers → about 37,000 scores.

in our kolam model 256 numbers → 11 scores, one each for 0–8, |, <end>: self.out = nn.Linear(d, len(VOCAB)), model.py:109.
14Softmaxtop · green

Turn scores into percentages

Softmax makes every score positive (eˣ) and divides by the total, so they add up to 100%. Bigger score → bigger share, and the order never changes.

simple example · drag the scores

The same softmax sits inside every orange box, turning match scores into mixing percentages. And a score of −∞ gives exactly 0%: that's how the mask of box 10 blocks.

in our kolam model In training, softmax hides inside the loss (F.cross_entropy, model.py:129). When reading an image the code skips it: the biggest score always gets the biggest share, so it just takes the biggest allowed score (argmax, model.py:149). Tokens that aren't allowed get −∞ first, the same trick as the mask.
15Output Probabilitiesthe very top

Pick a word, feed it back, repeat

Take the most likely word, add it to the answer, and send it back in at the bottom right (box 8). Repeat until <end>. That's the dashed loop in the picture.

simple example · press next
how it learns

Training scores the model by how surprised it was by the right word: loss = −log(p). It gave “noir” 81% → loss 0.21, small. Had it given 5% → loss 3.0, big. Training nudges every number in every box a little to make the loss smaller, over millions of examples. Nobody programs grammar in; it falls out of this game.

in our kolam model predict(), model.py:131: start with <bos>, run, take the best allowed token (allowed_next blocks impossible ones, like a wrong row length), append, stop at <end>. Lesson 1 replays this on a real kolam.

Which part of the picture the famous models keep

Left tower only. BERT, image models (ViT), our Model B. Reads, doesn't write.

Right tower only, no bridge. GPT and most chat models. Writes, never looks ahead.

Both. The 2017 translator, image captioners, and our Model A, with its left tower swapped for the CNN eye.

Now you know the words

token
one piece of input: a word, a word-piece, an image patch, or for us a digit like “6” or “|”.
vocabulary
the fixed list of every token the model knows: ~37,000 in the paper, 11 in ours.
vector · embedding
a list of numbers; the embedding is the vector a token gets from the table.
positional encoding
the “where am I” stamp added to each vector (the ⊕).
attention
each token searching the others and mixing in what it finds.
query · key · value
what I'm looking for · what I say I am · what I hand over.
head
one of several attentions running side by side, each with its own Q, K, V.
self / cross
attention within one list · the writer looking across at the reader (or the image).
mask
blocks a token from seeing later tokens: their scores become −∞, so 0%.
residual · Add
keep the original vector and add the box's change to it.
LayerNorm · Norm
rescale each vector to a steady size.
feed forward
a small network every token runs through alone: wide, ReLU, narrow.
layer · N×
one full block of boxes; the model stacks N of them.
encoder · decoder
the reader tower · the writer tower.
logits
the raw scores from Linear, one per token (11 per step in ours).
softmax
turns scores into percentages that add up to 100%.
teacher forcing
training the writer on the true answer shifted right, every slot at once.
cross-entropy
the loss: how surprised the model was by the right token, −log(p).
